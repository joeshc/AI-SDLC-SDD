I am an **AI Agents Developer** based in Chennai, Tamil Nadu, with more than eleven years in software. My work now centres on one idea, which is also the line in my profile: **build AI that drafts, and humans approve.**

That sentence is a design constraint rather than a slogan. In the domains I work in — healthcare above all — the interesting question is not how much a model can generate, but which decisions stay with an accountable person and how the system proves they did.

## What I work on

- **Agentic AI** — agents with defined purposes, tools, permissions and approval boundaries, rather than open-ended assistants.
- **RAG** — grounding answers in an organisation's own policies and documents, with access control and source attribution intact.
- **Workflow automation** — the operational plumbing that connects departments, where most enterprise defects actually live.
- **Healthcare systems** — hospital workflows end to end: registration, clinical documentation, diagnostics, pharmacy, billing and insurance.
- **Spec-driven development** — treating the specification as a versioned engineering artifact so AI-assisted work stays traceable to intent.

## How I approach AI in regulated domains

Clinical and financial workflows do not tolerate silent inference. The practices I keep returning to are the unglamorous ones:

- Write the requirement down, version it, and have a domain expert approve it before implementation begins.
- Give an agent tools, not database access, and put a policy gateway between the two.
- Make every accepted *and* rejected action auditable, because the rejected ones are what an investigation asks about.
- Keep diagnosis, prescribing, triage acuity and final interpretation with qualified clinicians, regardless of how capable the model becomes.
